Chapter one

Runs the room, then steps aside

Most of the evening it simply shows the church’s Check-in Display. At the right moment it becomes the lesson, and then it hands the room back.

No one has to remember to switch it over or update it. The kiosk keeps its own schedule, waits for a leader to press Begin, and never interrupts something the room is watching.

  • One screen, two jobs, no clock-watching

    It shows check-in until 6:30, turns to the lesson on its own, and goes back at 7:15. A leader can still switch it by hand at any time with one corner button.

    Peek under the hood: One screen, two jobs, no clock-watching
    The Pi’s own clock sets the switch (18:30 and 19:15). A check every 15 seconds only acts at those two moments, so a leader’s manual switch in between is never fought, and a reboot mid-evening comes back on the right screen.

  • A start screen, not a surprise

    Instead of a video suddenly starting at 6:30, the room sees a title card with the week and the lesson. The video begins when a leader presses Begin Video, the space bar or the right arrow.

    Peek under the hood: A start screen, not a surprise
    That press is also the browser’s permission to play sound, so the lesson starts unmuted. The video is lined up in the background the whole time; only playback waits.

  • Picks up where the lesson stopped

    If a showing is cut short (a power cut, a reboot, a switch back to check-in), the start screen offers to resume where it left off, to within a few seconds, or to start over.

    ▶Resume at 3:42 Start over
    Peek under the hood: Picks up where the lesson stopped
    About every 5 seconds the kiosk notes its place on the device. The offer appears only for the same week’s lesson, at least 30 seconds in, more than 10 seconds from the end, and less than four hours old. Otherwise it is plain Begin Video, because resuming the wrong video would be worse than starting over.

  • Updates itself, never mid-lesson

    When an improvement ships, the kiosk notices within a few minutes and reloads onto it by itself, with no one walking over to it. It never does this while a lesson, the slides or the settings are on screen.

    Peek under the hood: Updates itself, never mid-lesson
    Every 3 minutes it compares its own build with the site’s, and it checks that the new page is really being served before reloading, so it never lands back on the old one. A busy kiosk asks again every 15 seconds, with no deadline: an evening that never goes quiet simply updates tomorrow.

Chapter two

Built so the lesson lands

The video is the easy part. The care is in making sure every student in the room can follow it.

Captions a whole room can read, the teaching slides straight after, and small controls that keep a leader in charge of the pace.

  • Captions, screened for invented lines

    Every lesson video, Student and Leader, can show word-for-word captions. Lines that looked doubtful were checked against the actual audio, not just proofread.

    Peek under the hood: Captions, screened for invented lines
    Speech-to-text can invent a fluent sentence nobody said. A checker flags any line crammed in faster than anyone can speak (over 40 characters a second), or a near-copy of the line before it, and a week’s captions cannot be published until each of those is corrected or confirmed against a fresh, independent decode of that moment. Lines that are merely fast get a careful second read.

  • Asked once, then remembered

    The first time a lesson plays on a screen it asks “Show captions?” and remembers the answer, so club night is never interrupted by the question again.

    Peek under the hood: Asked once, then remembered
    The choice is kept on that one device. Afterwards the CC button on the control bar is the way to change it, which is why it is always visible and shows plainly whether captions are on.

  • Sized for a TV or a phone

    Captions are large enough to read across a room and still fit a phone held sideways. Each screen can choose Small, Normal, Large or Extra large, and a solid dark band for bright scenes.

    Peek under the hood: Sized for a TV or a phone
    The page draws the captions itself rather than leaving it to the browser, so they can be measured, kept off the control bar and sized from the screen (clamp(18px, 4.5vmin, 56px)). The size choice multiplies that one rule, so every option still suits every screen.

  • Teaching slides follow the video

    When the lesson ends, the week’s teaching slides come up, followed by closing slides for discussion: Talk About It, Remember This and This Week, written from that week’s lesson.

    Peek under the hood: Teaching slides follow the video
    The closing slides are real text laid over the deck’s own background, so they stay crisp and editable. Space, the right arrow or a tap moves on. Settings chooses which closing slides to add, and a video that fails outright, or stalls in its final seconds, still hands over to the slides instead of freezing.

  • Tonight’s bullets, in a leader’s own words

    A leader who wants different questions for their group can type new ones into Settings on the kiosk. One tap resets them to the written ones.

    Peek under the hood: Tonight’s bullets, in a leader’s own words
    Saved on that kiosk only, and only for the slides that were actually changed, so a later correction to the written bullets still reaches every slide the leader left alone. Up to three bullets of 80 characters each. Settings shows an “Edited on this device” badge, since the slide itself looks the same either way.

  • Straight to the slides

    Some nights the room has already seen the lesson, or there isn’t time for it. “Skip to slides” on the start screen goes straight to the discussion.

    Peek under the hood: Straight to the slides
    Shift plus the right arrow does the same. A plain right arrow still begins the video on purpose, so a reflexive key press can never skip a lesson.

  • Plays smoothly, even on the little PiEach lesson is prepared so even the little Pi Zero plays it smoothly, at a quality it can manage. A more capable screen gets full quality.

Chapter three

Prep made for a leader

A leader who has read ahead teaches with more confidence, so the kiosk doubles as a place to prepare.

The settings button in the corner also opens each week’s written prep, the full transcript and the leader handout, on the TV, or on a leader’s own phone before club.

  • Read Prep, on any screen

    Each week’s Leader Video has a short written prep: the big idea, key points, scripture and discussion questions, as real text that reads well on a phone, not a document stuck on the TV.

    Peek under the hood: Read Prep, on any screen
    Written from the same summaries as the printed handout, and kept on the device once loaded, so it opens even with the network down. The student side has no summary on purpose: the summaries were written from the Leader Video.

  • The full transcript, one tap from the video

    Under the summary sits the whole transcript, cleaned up for reading or in the exact spoken words. Tapping a paragraph’s timestamp plays the video from that moment.

    Peek under the hood: The full transcript, one tap from the video
    Both versions travel together, so switching fetches nothing. The edited version keeps every point, example and Scripture reference, and a check refuses to publish a week if a passage goes missing or a book of the Bible appears that the speaker never named.

  • Handouts that screen readers can follow

    Each week with a Leader Video has a printable handout, a summary page and the edited transcript, that opens full-screen right on the kiosk.

    Peek under the hood: Handouts that screen readers can follow
    A tagged PDF made from ordinary HTML, then stamped and checked for a title and a language setting, so assistive technology can move through it by heading.

  • Preview any week

    Settings lists all 32 lessons, so a leader can watch an upcoming week, catch up after a missed night or open just the slides, without changing what plays at 6:30.

    Peek under the hood: Preview any week
    While a preview runs, the schedule and the hourly lesson check stand aside, so neither can interrupt it. It always asks which one first (Student Video, Leader Video or just the teaching slides), and ending it hands back to whatever the schedule says.

Chapter four

Keeps working when the Wi-Fi doesn’t

A church connection on a club night is not a data center, and the room should never have to notice.

Most of the engineering here exists for one reason: so that a weak signal, a small computer or a busy evening never costs the students their lesson.

  • The week’s lesson is saved ahead

    This week’s video, captions, prep transcripts, handout and slides are saved ahead on the kiosk itself, well before 6:30, so a bad connection that evening doesn’t cost the lesson.

    Peek under the hood: The week’s lesson is saved ahead
    On start-up, every hour, and whenever the connection comes back. Last week’s lesson is cleared only after every piece of this week’s is safely stored, so a failed update never leaves the kiosk with nothing.

  • Streams first, with a safety net

    The lesson plays from the network when it can, because on the little Pi that plays most smoothly. If the stream stalls, the kiosk switches to the lesson it saved ahead, without losing the place.

    Peek under the hood: Streams first, with a safety net
    A bounded check (2.5s) decides whether to stream. A stall of 8 seconds switches to the saved lesson, once per showing, remembering the position, and never in the final seconds, where the hand-off to the slides takes over.

  • No button ever feels dead

    Every button and key visibly responds the instant it is pressed, even on a crawling connection, so a leader is never left wondering whether it worked.

    Peek under the hood: No button ever feels dead
    A hard rule in the code: no click or key press may wait on the network before something on screen changes, and anything a leader is waiting on has a time limit. It was learned live, on flaky Wi-Fi.

  • Stays awake between lessons

    The screen won’t drift off to sleep in the long quiet stretch before 6:30.

    Peek under the hood: Stays awake between lessons
    The page asks the browser to keep the screen on, and asks again whenever it comes back into view. The Pi setup guide also turns off screen blanking, as a backstop.

  • Right for a small Pi or a big screen

    The same page runs on the little Pi and on a laptop or TV elsewhere. On the Pi the lesson is tuned to play smoothly and the Check-in Display runs with its animations off. A capable computer gets full quality.

    Peek under the hood: Right for a small Pi or a big screen
    A Raspberry Pi, most phones and tablets, or a machine with two cores or fewer is treated as low power; the embedded display is opened with its ?lowPower=1 flag. Settings can choose Auto, Full quality or Low power for each device.

  • A loading screen that tells the truth

    If a video is still loading after about 12 seconds, the screen says the internet may be down and points to the way forward: on to the slides, or back to check-in.

    Peek under the hood: A loading screen that tells the truth
    A stalled connection sends no error, so an endless “Loading…” would look like progress. The switch-back button never waits on the network.

Smaller things worth knowing

  • Back or ahead 15 seconds with two buttons or the comma and period keys, so replaying one sentence never overshoots
  • Tap the video to pause, or press the space bar; the controls stay up while paused
  • The pointer and controls fade during the lesson and return with any movement or tap
  • Slides can turn themselves if a leader sets it, every 15 seconds to 2 minutes; by default they stay in a leader’s hands
  • One double-click goes fullscreen, the whole page, so the corner buttons never disappear with it
  • S opens Settings from anywhere, and Escape closes it
  • A finished lesson hands back early: Finish on the last slide returns to the Check-in Display without waiting for 7:15
  • A preview is always a one-off and never changes tonight’s scheduled lesson
